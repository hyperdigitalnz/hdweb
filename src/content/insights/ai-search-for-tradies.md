A homeowner in Hastings used to google "plumber hastings" and scroll the results. Now a
growing number of them type "who's a good plumber in Hastings?" into ChatGPT, or read the
AI answer Google puts above the normal results, and ring whoever the machine names. No
scrolling, no comparing ten websites. One answer, one or two businesses mentioned, done.

If your business is one of the names AI mentions, that's a lead you never had to bid for.
If it isn't, you're invisible in a channel that's only getting bigger. The good news:
getting recommended by AI is not magic, and most of your competitors haven't even thought
about it. This guide covers how AI search actually picks businesses, and the seven things
a NZ tradie can do about it.

## What AI search actually is

"AI search" covers a few different tools that all do the same job: answer the question
directly instead of showing a list of links.

- **Google AI Overviews and AI Mode.** The AI-written answer that now sits above the
  normal results on many searches. Same Google, new front door.
- **ChatGPT.** When you ask it something local or current, it searches the web and
  answers with sources.
- **Others.** Perplexity, Microsoft Copilot, Claude, Gemini. Different brands, same
  pattern: they read web pages, then write an answer that names and links a handful of
  sources.

You'll also see marketing people throw around new acronyms: **GEO (generative engine
optimisation)** and **AEO (answer engine optimisation)**. Both mean the same thing:
making your business the one AI answers cite and recommend. Don't let the jargon
intimidate you. It's a new name for a familiar job: being the obvious, credible answer
to "who should I hire?"

## How AI decides which tradie to recommend

None of these tools have opinions of their own. When someone asks for a sparky in
Napier, the AI goes looking at the same sources a careful customer would:

1. **Search indexes.** AI tools run web searches behind the scenes and read the top
   results. If you rank well in normal Google and Bing searches, you're in the pool of
   pages the AI reads. If you don't, you're mostly not.
2. **Your Google Business Profile and reviews.** For "near me" questions, AI answers
   lean heavily on maps data: who's nearby, who's open, who has strong recent reviews.
3. **Your website.** The AI reads your pages looking for plain statements it can lift:
   what you do, where you work, whether you handle the specific job being asked about.
4. **What others say about you.** Directory listings, supplier pages, local news, trade
   association memberships. Independent mentions make you look like an established
   business rather than a website making claims about itself.

Notice what that list is: local SEO. AI search doesn't replace the fundamentals, it
sits on top of them. A tradie with a fast website, clear service pages, a well-kept
Google Business Profile and a steady stream of reviews is already most of the way to
being AI-recommendable. That's also why you should be wary of anyone selling "GEO
packages" as if it's a separate dark art (more on that below).

## The reality check

Straight up: AI search is still a small slice of how tradies get found in New Zealand
today. Google's classic results and the map pack still drive most enquiries, and paid
Google Ads still puts you in front of people the day you turn it on. We run marketing
for dozens of NZ trade businesses and that's what the numbers say right now.

But the trend line only points one way. Google is putting AI answers on more searches
every month, and ChatGPT-style tools are becoming the default way a chunk of people
research anything, including who to hire. The tradies who tidy this up now are building
a lead source their competitors don't know exists yet. The work below is cheap, mostly
one-off, and doubles as ordinary good SEO, so nothing is wasted even if AI search grows
slower than expected.

## The seven-step plan

### 1. Get your Google Business Profile match fit

For local "who should I call" questions, your Google Business Profile does more work
than your website. Make sure it's claimed, and then fill in everything: exact services,
service areas, hours, photos of real jobs, and a description written in plain English.
Then keep reviews coming. Recent, detailed reviews that mention the job and the suburb
("replaced our hot water cylinder in Havelock North, same week") are exactly the kind
of evidence AI answers repeat. If asking for reviews feels awkward, use our
[copy-paste review templates](/insights/ask-for-google-reviews/) to make it a habit.

### 2. Make your business details identical everywhere

AI cross-references. If your website says "Smith Plumbing Ltd", your Facebook says
"Smith Plumbing & Gas" and an old Yellow listing has your previous phone number, you
look uncertain, and uncertain businesses don't get recommended. Pick one exact business
name, address and phone number, and sweep every listing you can find until they match:
Google, Facebook, directories, your email signature, the lot.

### 3. Have a page for each service you want to be found for

AI can only recommend you for drain unblocking if something it reads says you do drain
unblocking. A homepage that says "quality plumbing services" tells the machine almost
nothing. What works is a simple page per main service and per main area, with plain
first-sentence answers: what the job is, where you do it, roughly how it works. This is
the same structure that wins normal Google rankings, which is exactly the point. If
your current site can't do this, that's the first thing to fix, and it's the core of
[how lead generation works for tradies](/insights/lead-generation-for-tradies/) whether
AI is involved or not.

### 4. Answer real customer questions on your pages

AI answers are built by extraction: the tool grabs clear question-and-answer passages
from pages it trusts. So give it some. Add a short FAQ to each service page answering
the questions customers actually ask you: "How much does it cost to reroof a 3-bedroom
house?", "Do you charge a call-out fee?", "Are you licensed for gasfitting?" One
question, one direct answer in the first sentence, a bit of detail after. Vague answers
written to hide your prices get skipped; specific ranges get quoted.

### 5. Get mentioned in places AI reads

Independent mentions are votes. The easy wins for a tradie: your trade association
listing (Master Plumbers, Master Electricians, Certified Builders and the like), the
main NZ directories, supplier or manufacturer "find an installer" pages, local business
awards, and any local news or community coverage you can get. You don't need hundreds
of these. A handful of legitimate, consistent mentions separates you from the
competitor whose only web presence is their own website.

### 6. Don't accidentally block the AI

This one bites people silently. AI tools send crawlers (with names like GPTBot,
ClaudeBot and PerplexityBot) to read your website, and it's now common for websites to
block them without the owner ever knowing. Cloudflare, which sits in front of a huge
share of NZ websites, offers a "block AI bots" setting that does exactly what it says:
tick it and ChatGPT can no longer read your site. We've seen it switched on by default
on new setups. Ask whoever manages your website to check your robots.txt file and any
"AI bot" settings, and make sure the crawlers you want (the ones attached to AI search
tools) are allowed. While they're in there, they can add an llms.txt file, a small
plain-text summary of your business written for AI tools. It's optional and new, but it
costs ten minutes.

### 7. Test it, then fix what you find

The best audit is free: open ChatGPT and ask it what a customer would. "Who's a good
roofer in Tauranga?" "Is [your business name] any good?" "Who should I call for a heat
pump install in Christchurch?" Try Google's AI results and Perplexity too. Three things
can happen: you're recommended (great, note why: usually reviews or a specific page),
you're missing (work through steps 1 to 6), or the AI says something wrong about you,
like an old address or a service you don't offer (find the page it got that from and
correct it at the source). Re-test every month or two. It's the closest thing AI search
has to a rank tracker.

## What not to bother with

A new channel always attracts snake oil, so, disclosure first: we're a marketing agency
that sells this kind of work, and even we think you should skip most of what's being
sold under the AI-search banner right now.

- **Don't pay for a separate "GEO retainer" on top of SEO.** If the deliverables list
  looks like relabelled SEO (it usually does), it should be part of your existing SEO
  work, not a second invoice.
- **Don't stuff pages with "best plumber" self-praise.** AI tools weigh what others say
  about you far more than what you say about yourself. Chest-beating copy reads as
  noise to humans and machines alike.
- **Don't buy fake reviews or directory spam.** The platforms filter it, and a profile
  with 40 five-star reviews and no photos of real work convinces no one, silicon or
  otherwise.
- **Don't drop Google Ads or SEO to chase AI.** Today they bring the leads; AI search
  is the channel you set up alongside them. If you're weighing those up, start with
  [Google Ads vs SEO for tradies](/insights/google-ads-vs-seo-for-tradies/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is GEO different from SEO?

Mostly no. Generative engine optimisation and answer engine optimisation both boil down
to being crawlable, clearly written, well reviewed and independently mentioned, which
is what good local SEO already does. The extra AI-specific work (allowing AI crawlers,
adding an llms.txt file, testing what AI tools say about you) is real but small.

### Do I need an llms.txt file?

It's a nice-to-have, not a must. It's a simple text file that gives AI tools a clean
summary of your business and services. Cheap insurance, minor factor. Fix your Google
Business Profile and service pages first.

### How long does it take to show up in AI answers?

There's no queue to join and no guaranteed timeline. AI tools re-read the web
constantly, so a fixed profile, new reviews or a corrected page can show up in answers
within weeks, but nobody can promise a date. Anyone who guarantees "you'll be in
ChatGPT in 30 days" is guessing at best.

### How do I check if ChatGPT recommends my business?

Ask it, the same way a customer would: "who's a good [your trade] in [your town]?" and
"is [your business] any good?" Check Google's AI results and Perplexity while you're at
it. Do it monthly and keep a note of what changes.

### Is AI search actually sending tradies leads in NZ yet?

Some, and growing. It's still small next to Google search and the map pack, and those
same fundamentals feed both. That's the practical takeaway: nothing on this list is
wasted, because it strengthens the channels bringing your leads today and positions you
for the one arriving next.
