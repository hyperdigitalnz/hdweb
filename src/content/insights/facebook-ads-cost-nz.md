Almost every article about Facebook ad costs quotes global averages from an American blog.
Those numbers are close to useless here, because you are not bidding against American
advertisers for a New Zealand audience.

So here are real figures from a live New Zealand Meta account: ours.

Our interest, stated plainly: we run Meta ads for trade businesses, so we are selling
something. The numbers below are straight out of our own ad account for the 90 days to
**18/08/2026**, unedited, and we will be clear about exactly what they do and do not tell
you.

## The real numbers

Across three campaigns, roughly $1,900 of spend, 67,000 impressions and 1,120 clicks:

| Metric | Range |
|---|---|
| **CPM** (cost per 1,000 impressions) | $20.06 to $30.58 |
| **CPC** (cost per click) | $1.25 to $2.29 |
| **Cost per link click** | $2.84 to $4.54 |
| **Click-through rate** | 1.08% to 1.89% |

**The important caveat.** This is our account, advertising to trade business owners. That
is a narrow B2B audience, and narrow audiences cost more per thousand impressions than
broad ones.

A plumber advertising to homeowners in Hamilton is targeting a much wider audience, and
will usually see **lower** CPMs than the figures above. Treat our numbers as a ceiling for
a local trade business, not a forecast. The shape of the numbers is what transfers, not
the exact figures.

## What "cost" actually means on Facebook

There is no price list. Meta runs an auction, and what you pay is decided by who else
wants the same eyeballs at the same moment.

That means three different people quoting you "the cost of Facebook ads" can all be right,
because they are quoting different metrics:

- **CPM** is what you pay per thousand times your ad is shown. This is the number that
  reflects how contested your audience is.
- **CPC** is what you pay per click. Driven by your CPM *and* how compelling your ad is.
  A better ad gets more clicks from the same impressions, so your CPC falls.
- **Cost per lead** is the only one that matters commercially, and it depends as much on
  your landing page and your follow-up as on the ad.

You can have a cheap CPM and a terrible cost per lead. That happens constantly, and it is
usually the landing page, not the ads.

## What drives your cost up or down

**How narrow your targeting is.** The tighter the audience, the higher the CPM. Ours is
narrow, which is why our CPMs sit where they do.

**The time of year.** November and December are the dearest months of the year on Meta,
because every retailer in the country is bidding for the same attention. A trade business
advertising in October pays less than one advertising in late November for the same
audience.

**How good the creative is.** This is the biggest lever you control. Meta rewards ads
people actually engage with by showing them more cheaply. A genuinely good ad can halve
your effective cost, and a bad one is expensive no matter how well the account is set up.

**Whether you are asking for too much too soon.** Cold audiences who have never heard of
you convert worse than people who have seen your work. That is what retargeting is for.

## What a tradie should budget

Some straight guidance rather than a range designed to make the number look small.

**Below about $10 a day, Meta struggles.** The system needs enough events to learn who to
show your ads to. Very small budgets spend the whole month in the learning phase and never
settle.

**$300 to $600 a month of media** is a realistic starting point for a local trade
business, and roughly where our own entry package sits. That buys enough impressions in a
single region to tell within a couple of months whether it is working.

**Give it at least 8 weeks.** Anyone judging Meta ads on two weeks of data is reading
noise. Costs swing week to week and the first fortnight is the algorithm working you out.

**Budget for the landing page too.** Sending paid traffic to a slow site or a page with no
clear next step is the single most common way tradies waste money on Meta. Our guide to
[what a website costs in NZ](/insights/website-cost-nz/) covers that side.

## Facebook or Google for a trade business?

Different jobs, and for most trades the answer is Google first.

**Google catches people already looking.** Someone typing "emergency plumber Napier" has
the problem right now. That intent is why Google clicks cost more and convert better. Our
[Google Ads cost guide](/insights/google-ads-cost-nz/) has the equivalent numbers.

**Meta creates demand rather than catching it.** Nobody is scrolling Instagram hoping to
find a roofer. Meta works for trades when the job is visual and somewhat discretionary:
decks, landscaping, kitchens, painting, heat pumps before winter. It also works very well
for staying in front of people who already looked at your site.

If you are choosing one, choose Google. If you are running both, Meta is where you spend
the money that Google's limited search volume cannot absorb.

## Facebook ads cost FAQ

### How much do Facebook ads cost in New Zealand?

From our own live account in the 90 days to 18/08/2026: CPMs of $20 to $31, clicks of
$1.25 to $2.29, and link clicks of $2.84 to $4.54. Ours targets a narrow B2B audience, so
a local trade business advertising to homeowners will usually pay less per thousand
impressions.

### What is a good cost per lead for a tradie?

It depends entirely on what a job is worth to you. Work out your average job value and
your close rate, and the answer falls out: if a job is worth $2,000 and you close one in
four leads, a $150 lead is comfortably profitable. Chasing the cheapest possible lead
usually buys worse leads.

### Is a $5 a day Facebook budget worth it?

Rarely. Meta needs enough conversion events to optimise, and $5 a day generally does not
produce them. You are better saving it and running $300 over a single month than dribbling
it out across three.

### Do Instagram ads cost more than Facebook?

They come from the same auction and the same budget, and Meta places your ad wherever it
expects the better result. Costs differ by placement but you are not really choosing
between them. Let it optimise across both unless you have a strong reason not to.

### Why did my costs suddenly jump?

Usually one of three things: your creative has fatigued (the same people have seen it too
many times), you are in a competitive season like the run-up to Christmas, or your audience
is too small and you have exhausted it. Fresh creative fixes the first, patience fixes the
second, wider targeting fixes the third.

### Can I run Facebook ads myself?

Yes, and the platform is designed to let you. Where most tradies come unstuck is not the
setup, it is the follow-up: the enquiry arrives and sits unanswered until the weekend.
Whoever replies first usually wins the job, so fix that before you spend a dollar.
