Every tradie in New Zealand gets pitched leads from somewhere: directories, lead
resellers, agencies, "guaranteed" Facebook systems, a cousin who knows SEO. Most of the
pitches leave out the only two questions that matter: what does a *won job* actually
cost through this channel, and who owns the asset your money is building?

This guide covers every channel that meaningfully produces work for NZ trade businesses
in 2026, with real numbers where we have them (we manage marketing for trade businesses
across the country, and we publish our data). Fair warning about our bias up front: we
build owned pipelines for a living. The facts below are checkable either way.

## The only framework you need: rented leads vs owned leads

Every channel is one of two kinds.

**Rented:** a platform finds the customer and sells you access. Fast to start, nothing
to build, and the asset (the rankings, the reviews, the customer relationship) belongs
to the platform. Stop paying, and it's like you were never there.

**Owned:** the customer finds *you*: your website, your Google profile, your reviews,
your name. Slower to build and it costs real money up front, but every dollar compounds
into something that keeps producing after the spend stops.

Strong trade businesses usually start with some rented volume and deliberately build
toward owned. The trap is renting forever and calling it a pipeline.

## The channels, one by one

### Job marketplaces (Builderscrack and friends)

Homeowners post jobs, tradies pay to connect and quote, usually against several others.
Fastest way to real quoting opportunities from a standing start, and the racing keeps
your pricing sharp. The catch is the maths of shared leads: you pay per connection, not
per win. We've pulled the model apart in [our Builderscrack
review](/insights/is-builderscrack-worth-it/). Rented.

### Ratings directories (NoCowboys and similar)

A flat yearly fee for a prominent, reviewed profile where homeowners go to check up on
tradies. Closer to a reputation billboard than a lead machine, and occasionally worth
it when their category page outranks anything you could build soon. The breakdown:
[is NoCowboys worth it?](/insights/is-nocowboys-worth-it/). Rented.

### Lead resellers

Companies that generate enquiries (usually through their own ads) and sell them to you,
sometimes exclusively, sometimes not. Judge them exactly one way: cost per *won* job
over a real sample, against the same money in your own ads. Ask whether the lead is
exclusive, how old it is, and what happens to your money when a lead was never real.
Rented, with the extra wrinkle that you're buying from someone else's ad account you
can't see.

### Google Ads

The fastest owned channel: your ad, your website, your exclusive enquiry, live within
days. For most NZ trades a click costs roughly $2 to $6.50 regionally, more in the main
centres; we publish the full table in [what Google Ads costs in
NZ](/insights/google-ads-cost-nz/). It only works as well as the website behind it, and
badly-run accounts burn money on junk searches, which is where the horror stories come
from. Owned (provided the account is in your name, which you should insist on).

### Your Google Business Profile, reviews and local SEO

The free channel almost every tradie under-uses. Your Google Business Profile *is* your
map-pack listing, and review count and quality are among the factors deciding who shows
up in those top three spots, as well as being the social proof that wins the click. The
asking costs nothing and takes ten minutes to set up: [copy-paste templates
here](/insights/ask-for-google-reviews/), and [how to keep them
coming](/insights/get-more-google-reviews/) if the asking is the bit you find awkward.
[Ranking in the map pack](/insights/rank-in-google-map-pack/) covers what decides the order
once your profile is complete. Website-side local SEO compounds on top over months.
Slowest to build, cheapest per lead once it's running. Owned, permanently.

### Facebook and Instagram

Search catches people who already need you; Meta creates demand among people who
don't yet. It suits work people plan ahead for (renovations, heat pumps, decks,
painting) and is weak for urgent call-outs, because nobody with a burst pipe is
scrolling. Usually the second paid channel, not the first. [How we run
it](/facebook-ads/). Owned.

### Referrals, repeat work and follow-up

Still the best leads in the business, and the most mismanaged. Three habits do most of
the work: reply to every enquiry fast (the fastest reply usually wins the job), chase
every quote until it's a yes or a no ([template and reasons
here](/insights/quote-template-nz/)), and ask every happy customer for a review. None of
it costs a marketing dollar; all of it compounds. Owned.

## The sensible order (if you're starting from scratch)

1. **Claim and complete your Google Business Profile, and start asking for reviews.**
   Free, and it improves every other channel's conversion.
2. **Get a website that converts**: fast on a phone, one page per service, tap-to-call,
   a short quote form, an instant reply to every enquiry. ([What that should
   cost.](/insights/website-cost-nz/))
3. **Turn on Google Ads** for your best services and areas. Speed while everything else
   grows, and real data on which searches turn into jobs.
4. **Let SEO and reviews compound** on the pages and profile you now have.
5. **Add Meta** if your work is the planned-ahead kind.
6. **Use marketplaces deliberately**, to fill gaps, not as the foundation.

## The multiplier nobody budgets for: follow-up

Whatever the channel, the leads go to whoever answers. An enquiry replied to instantly,
a quote chased until it's decided, a review requested after every job: these turn the
same lead flow into more booked work without buying another click. It's why we wire
[automatic replies and follow-up](/ai-automation/) into every system we build, and it's
the first thing to fix before spending more on any channel above.

## Tradie lead generation FAQ

### Is it worth paying for leads?

Paying for leads is fine; overpaying for *shared* leads forever is the trap. Work out
your cost per won job on any paid channel after a fair sample, compare it against
exclusive enquiries from your own ads, and let the numbers decide. Then make sure some
of your spend is always building the owned side.

### How do tradies get leads without paying?

Google Business Profile, reviews, referrals and follow-up. None of them cost money;
all of them cost consistency. A tradie with 40 strong Google reviews and same-day
replies out-generates plenty of businesses with ad budgets.

### How much does lead generation cost in NZ?

By channel: reviews and referrals cost time; Google Ads clicks run about $2 to $6.50
for most trades regionally (more in metros); marketplaces charge per connection;
directories charge flat fees ($999/yr in NoCowboys' case); managed systems like ours
run from $497 +GST a month plus your ad budget. Cost per won job is the number that
makes them comparable.

### What's the best lead generation for tradies?

The boring answer is the true one: the search-shaped channels first (ads plus map
pack), because they catch people already looking for your trade, then demand creation
and marketplaces around them. The best *single* move for most tradies is finishing
their Google Business Profile and building reviews, because it's free and lifts
everything else.
