If you run a trade business in New Zealand you have almost certainly come across
Profitable Tradie. The podcast, the free book, the Facebook ads, a mate who joined and
won't stop talking about it. So the question comes up: is it worth it?

Here is our interest, stated up front so you can weigh everything below. We are a
marketing agency for trade businesses. Profitable Tradie coaches trade business owners,
and part of what they coach is marketing. So we are adjacent to them and, for some
readers, an alternative to them. We are not going to trash a company we compete with at
the edges, because that would be both unfair and obvious. What follows sticks to
verifiable facts and a straight description of who each approach suits.

## What Profitable Tradie actually is

A business coaching and training company for trades business owners, founded by **Tony
Fraser-Jones**, headquartered at Level One, 851 Victoria Street, **Hamilton**, and
operating across New Zealand, Australia, the USA and Canada.

The offer is structured in three streams by business stage, per their own site:

- **Getting Started**, for new and small businesses
- **Fix My Business**, aimed at owners who are overworked and underpaid
- **Scale & Grow**, for businesses ready to expand

Around that sits a large free content operation: *The Profitable Tradie* book (a 118 page,
8 chapter book they give away), a podcast well past 100 episodes, plus calculators,
templates and scripts. The free material is genuinely free and genuinely useful, which is
worth saying plainly. It is also the top of a sales funnel, which is fine, but it is
useful to know which one you are in.

The coaching itself covers pricing, cash flow, hiring, team performance, sales, marketing
and the systems that let an owner step off the tools. That is a broader remit than
marketing alone.

## What it costs

**They do not publish prices.** We looked, and there is no pricing page. You get the
number on a call after a conversation about your business.

That is normal for coaching and consulting, and it is not by itself a criticism. Prices
genuinely do vary by programme and business size. It does mean you cannot compare it
against anything on paper before you speak to a salesperson, so go into that call knowing
your own numbers: what a job is worth to you, what your current profit actually is, and
what you could afford monthly without hurting.

If anyone reading this has been quoted, the useful thing to know is your own break-even.
Whatever the figure turns out to be, the question is the same as with any supplier: how
many extra jobs, or how much extra margin per job, does this need to produce before it
pays for itself?

## What the numbers on their website mean

Their site carries several headline claims. As at 17/08/2026 the homepage stated an
average profit generated of **$94,000** per business after 12 months, a reduction in
working hours of **12 hours**, **1,600** members helped across four countries, and
elsewhere invites you to "join over 4,271 trades and contracting businesses worldwide".
Other versions of these figures circulate too, including a $96,421 average profit and 14.7
hours a week.

Two things to hold in mind, neither of which is an accusation.

**These are self-reported and unaudited.** That is true of essentially every coaching
company's results claims, ours included when we quote client numbers. No independent body
verifies them.

**Averages hide the distribution.** An average of $94,000 tells you nothing about how many
members got nothing. If a handful of large businesses have a very good year, the average
climbs while the median member's experience stays flat. This is not a knock on Profitable
Tradie specifically, it is how averages work everywhere, and it is the right question to
ask any supplier quoting one. Ask what the median member gets, and what share do not
complete the programme.

## What members actually say

Here is what we found, or rather did not: **there is very little independent review
material.** We went
looking for it.

Their own site carries member success stories. Their social channels carry testimonials.
On Indeed there is a 4.6 out of 5 rating from 10 reviews, but those are **employee**
reviews, not customer ones, so they tell you about the workplace and nothing about the
programme. Beyond scattered forum threads, there is no substantial body of independent
customer reviews for the programme in New Zealand.

That is not evidence against them. Coaching clients rarely write public reviews, and a
company can be excellent with a thin review trail. But it does mean you cannot outsource
the diligence to strangers on the internet. If you are considering it, the highest-value
thing you can do is ask them to put you in touch with two or three current members in your
trade and your country, then ring them and ask what changed.

## Who coaching genuinely suits

Some tradies should absolutely do this, and we would say so on a call.

**You are busy but not making money.** Full calendar, empty bank account. That is a
pricing, quoting and job-costing problem. No amount of marketing fixes it, and more leads
will make it worse. Coaching is the right tool.

**You are the bottleneck.** Still on the tools, quoting at 9pm, everything routed through
you. Getting out of that is systems and delegation work, which is what a coach does.

**You need accountability.** Plenty of owners know what to do and don't do it. Paying
someone to hold you to it is a legitimate use of money, and arguably the main thing
coaching sells.

**You want to learn to run the business, not rent a supplier.** Coaching builds capability
that stays with you. That is a real, lasting asset.

## Who it doesn't suit

**Your problem is genuinely not enough enquiries.** If your systems are fine, your pricing
is fine, and the phone just is not ringing, coaching teaches you a marketing plan you then
still have to execute, on evenings you do not have. That is the wrong tool for that job.

**You have no time at all.** Coaching is work. Calls, homework, implementation. If you
cannot commit hours to it, you will pay for a year and get a login.

**You want it handled.** Coaching is by definition not done-for-you.

## The real decision: coaching or done-for-you

This is the fork, and it is worth being clear about because it is where our interest lies.

**Coaching teaches you to do it.** You learn pricing, sales, systems and marketing. You do
the work, or your team does. It builds capability you keep, and it costs you time.

**Done-for-you means someone else does it.** An agency runs the marketing, you run the
business. It costs money instead of time, and the capability sits with the supplier.

Neither is better. They fix different problems, and plenty of successful trade businesses
do both, with a coach on the business and an agency on the marketing.

The question that decides it is: **what is actually broken?**

- If the business is disorganised, underpriced or owner-dependent, coaching first. More
  leads into a broken business make everything worse.
- If the business runs well and simply needs more work coming in, marketing first.
- If you have time but not money, learn it. If you have money but not time, buy it.

For what it is worth, we tell people to fix pricing before they spend a dollar with us.
An extra ten jobs a month at the wrong price is a faster way to go broke than staying
quiet.

## Alternatives in New Zealand

**Do it yourself.** Their book is free and their podcast is free. A motivated owner can
get a long way on that alone before paying anyone. Start there.

**Other coaching.** Profitable Tradie is not the only trades coaching operation working in
New Zealand and Australia. Shop around, and ask each for member references in your trade.

**An industry association or peer group.** Cheaper, less structured, often underrated for
the accountability part.

**A marketing agency, us included.** If the gap is enquiries rather than operations, this
is the direct route. We build the website, run the ads and handle the follow-up. Our
prices are published on our [SEO](/seo/) and plans pages rather than quoted on a call,
which we think you should expect from anyone.

**Your accountant.** Genuinely. If the problem is that you do not know your numbers, an
afternoon with a good accountant is the cheapest version of this whole conversation.

## Profitable Tradie FAQ

### Is Profitable Tradie legitimate?

Yes. It is an established company with a physical Hamilton head office, a named leadership
team, staff, a long-running podcast and members across four countries. Whether the
programme is worth its price for your business is a separate question from whether the
company is real, and it is the one worth focusing on.

### How much does Profitable Tradie cost?

They do not publish pricing. You get a figure on a call after discussing your business.
Work out your own break-even before that call so you can judge the number against
something.

### Is the Profitable Tradie book worth reading?

It is free and it is aimed squarely at the problems most trade business owners have, so
there is very little reason not to. Read it before you buy anything from anyone, including
us.

### Profitable Tradie or a marketing agency?

Depends what is broken. Underpriced, disorganised or owner-dependent means coaching.
Well-run but too quiet means marketing. If you are not sure which, that is exactly what a
diagnostic conversation is for, and both we and they will give you one free.

### Do they guarantee results?

Check the current terms with them directly rather than trusting any blog, including this
one. Any guarantee worth having is written into the agreement, so ask to see it before you
sign.

### Are their published results real?

They are self-reported and unaudited, like almost all coaching results. That does not make
them false. Ask for the median rather than the average, and for references in your trade.
