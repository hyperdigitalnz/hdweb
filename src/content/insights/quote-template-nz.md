## What a professional NZ quote includes

The difference between a quote that protects you and a number in a text message:

- **A validity window.** "Valid for 30 days" means the price of timber in November is not
  your problem in February. Without it, an old quote can come back to bite you at last
  year's prices.
- **What's included, spelled out.** Labour, the materials you've listed, cleanup,
  whatever you're covering. If you lodge the consent, say so.
- **What's excluded, spelled out.** Consent fees, reinstating the garden, removing the
  old structure. Every dispute you've ever had about a job lives in this gap, so close it
  in writing.
- **A variations line.** One sentence: any work outside this scope gets quoted and agreed
  in writing before it's done. This is the single most profitable sentence in the
  template. Scope creep eats margins precisely because nobody wrote this down.
- **Payment terms.** Deposit on acceptance, progress payments on longer jobs, balance on
  completion. Agreeing this at quote time is easy; raising it after the job is awkward.
- **GST shown properly.** Quote the subtotal, the GST and the total. Surprising a
  homeowner with 15% at invoice time is a fight you booked weeks earlier.
- **A way to accept.** "Reply to this email or sign and return." A quote someone can
  accept in ten seconds gets accepted more often.

## How to set your charge-out rate

The most common pricing mistake in the trades is charging what the tradie down the road
charges. His overheads aren't yours, and neither is his mortgage. Your rate comes out of
your own numbers:

1. **What you need to earn.** Say $90,000 a year.
2. **What the business costs to run.** Ute, tools, insurance, phone, software,
   accountant. Say $25,000. That's $115,000 to recover.
3. **Hours you can actually bill.** A 40-hour week for 46 weeks is 1,840 hours, but
   quoting, travel, admin and the odd quiet patch mean most solo tradies bill 60 to 70%
   of that. Call it 1,200 hours.
4. **Divide.** $115,000 over 1,200 hours is roughly $96 an hour just to break even.
5. **Add a margin.** 15 to 20% on top for profit, risk and the quiet months. That lands
   you around $110 to $115 an hour, plus GST.

Run your own numbers through those five steps. If the answer is higher than what you're
charging now, that gap is why busy doesn't feel like profitable. And if a customer says
you're dearer than the next guy: he's not paying your overheads, and one of you has done
this maths.

## Price the whole job, not just the obvious bits

The labour and the big materials always make it into the quote. The margin leaks are
everything else:

- **Materials margin.** Charging materials at cost turns you into a free purchasing
  service. A 10 to 20% handling margin on materials is standard practice: you're funding
  the purchase, collecting it, carrying the risk and sorting the returns.
- **Travel and delivery runs.** Forty minutes each way, every day, for a week is a full
  billable day.
- **Consumables.** Blades, fixings, gas, drop cloths. Small per job, real per year.
- **Disposal.** Skip hire and dump fees on the jobs that need them.
- **The awkward tenth hour.** If you reckon a job at 8 hours, quote what it takes when
  something goes sideways, because something usually does.

The template's line-item layout makes this easy: separate lines for labour, materials and

## Quotes are won in the follow-up

Here's the part almost nobody does. You price the job, send it, and wait. The customer
meanwhile has three quotes, a busy week and no urgency. The tradie who checks in a few
days later, answers the one lingering question and makes saying yes easy is very often
the one who gets the job, and it's frequently not the cheapest quote that wins.

## Quote template FAQ

### Is there a free quote template in Word?

Yes, the download above is a .docx that opens in Word, Google Docs and LibreOffice. Fill
in your details once and save it as your master copy.

### What's the best format for a quote?

Send a PDF. It can't be edited, it looks the same on every device, and it prints
cleanly. Keep the editable version for yourself. Same rule as invoices.

### How do you write a construction or building quote?

Describe the job in one plain sentence, itemise labour, materials and equipment, show
subtotal, GST and total, then state what's included, what's excluded, your variations
rule and your payment terms, with a validity window. That structure is the whole
template above.

### Can ChatGPT write my quotes?

It can draft the words, but it doesn't know your costs, your billable hours or what the
job is worth in your region, and that's the part that decides whether you make money. Use
the template for structure, your own numbers for pricing, and AI for tidying up wording
if you like.

### Should I charge for quotes?

For most residential work, free quotes are the market norm in NZ, and charging will cost
you enquiries. The exception is complex jobs that take real design or measure-up time,
where a paid detailed quote (often credited back if you win the job) is fair and filters
out tyre-kickers. Say which it is up front.

### How long should a quote stay valid?

Thirty days is standard. Shorter (14 days) if material prices are moving on you, and say
so in the quote: it's a genuine reason and a gentle nudge to decide.
