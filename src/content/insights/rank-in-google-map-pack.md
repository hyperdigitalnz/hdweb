You've claimed the profile. You've picked the categories, added the photos, filled in the
services. You search "plumber Napier" and you're sitting somewhere on page one, well below
the three businesses in the map at the top.

Those three get the phone calls. Everyone else is arguing over the leftovers.

This is the part nobody explains properly: filling in your Google Business Profile gets you
into the running, but it doesn't decide the race. If you've already done the setup work (and
if you haven't, start with our [Google Business Profile
checklist](/insights/google-business-profile-optimisation/)), this is what actually decides
who Google puts in those three slots, and what to do when it isn't you.

## First: make sure you're actually not ranking

Most tradies who tell us they "rank number one" are looking at a search they did from their
own yard.

The map pack is location-aware in a way normal search results aren't. Google knows roughly
where the phone is standing and builds the three results around that point. Search from your
workshop and you will very often see yourself at the top, because you are the closest
business to yourself. Drive fifteen minutes across town, search the same words, and you can
be nowhere.

Incognito doesn't fix this. It strips your search history, not your location.

**How to check properly:**

- Ask three customers or mates in different suburbs to search your main service and send you
  a screenshot. Free, and it's real data from real devices.
- Or use a grid tracking tool (Local Falcon and BrightLocal both do this cheaply). They
  search from dozens of pinned points across your area and hand you a heat map: green where
  you're in the three, red where you're invisible.

That map is the single most useful thing you can put in front of yourself, because it turns
"we're not ranking" into "we're strong in Napier, dead in Hastings", which is a problem you
can actually do something about.

## The proximity problem, explained

Google's three ranking factors are relevance, distance and prominence. Distance is the one
that quietly wrecks tradie expectations.

You do not rank in a town. You rank in a radius, and it shrinks as competition thickens. In
a city with forty electricians, the map pack for "electrician near me" changes every few
suburbs. In a rural area with three, one profile can cover fifty kilometres.

For a service area business (you travel to customers, no shopfront) that has hard limits.
Google still anchors you to your verified address even when it hides it. Listing Napier,
Hastings, Havelock North and Waipukurau as service areas tells Google where you'll go. It
does not plant a second pin in each town, and no amount of optimisation will make it.

So when the grid map shows you red in the next town over, you have three honest options:

1. **Earn a bigger radius.** More prominence (reviews, mentions, a stronger website) widens
   the area where you can beat the locals. Slow, real, and the only organic answer.
2. **Cover the gap with paid.** [Google Ads](/google-ads/) has no proximity ceiling. If
   Hastings is worth money to you and you can't rank there, buy the visibility while you
   build it. Our [Google Ads vs SEO](/insights/google-ads-vs-seo-for-tradies/) piece covers
   which one to fund first.
3. **Open a genuine second location.** Real address, real staff, real signage. Only if it's
   a real branch. Fake or virtual offices get profiles suspended, and they do get caught.

## The levers that actually move you

Once distance is understood, here's where the effort pays, roughly in order of return for a
trade business.

### 1. Your primary category

In Whitespark's annual survey of local SEOs, the primary category comes out at or near the
top of the list every year, and it stays there in the 2026 report. It's also a two-minute
change.

"Plumber", "Drainage service", "Hot water system supplier" and "Gasfitter" are four separate
categories that surface for four different sets of searches. Pick the one matching the work
you most want to be called for as primary, then add the rest as secondary.

Look at the businesses currently in the three. Their categories are public. If all three are
"Electrician" and you're primary-listed as "Electrical installation service", that alone can
be your problem.

### 2. Reviews, and what's inside them

Reviews feed prominence, but the 2026 picture is more specific than "get more stars":

- **Recency and cadence beat raw volume.** A steady trickle reads as an active business.
  Forty reviews that all landed in one fortnight three years ago do not.
- **Owner responses now carry real weight.** Reply to every review, good and bad. It's
  minutes of work and one of the few signals fully in your control.
- **The words in the review matter.** Google reads review text. A review saying "sorted our
  hot water cylinder in Taradale, same day" is relevance for hot water cylinders in Taradale
  in a way "great job, highly recommend" never is.

You can't script that last one, and you shouldn't try. What you can do is ask at the moment
the customer can still remember what you fixed, which is most of the battle. That's the
whole point of [asking without begging](/insights/get-more-google-reviews/), and the
[wording that works](/insights/ask-for-google-reviews/) is already written for you.

### 3. The website sitting behind the profile

This is the gap between the tradies who plateau and the ones who don't. Your profile links
to your website, and Google reads that website to work out how relevant you are.

A one-page site saying "Plumbing services, Hawke's Bay" gives it almost nothing. What helps:

- A real page per service, using the words customers use ("blocked drains", "hot water
  cylinder replacement", "switchboard upgrades").
- Pages for the towns you genuinely work in, with genuinely different content on each. Not
  the same paragraph with the place name swapped, which Google has been ignoring for years.
- Your name, address and phone number in the footer, matching the profile exactly.
- A site that loads fast on a phone on rural data.

Our [SEO and local](/seo/) page covers how the site and the profile work as one thing rather
than two.

### 4. Consistent details everywhere else

Citations are just your business details listed on other sites. In New Zealand the ones
worth having are the free obvious ones: Yellow, Localist, Neighbourly, your Facebook page,
your industry body (Master Plumbers, Master Electricians, Certified Builders), and the
directories you're already paying for, like [Builderscrack](/insights/is-builderscrack-worth-it/)
or [NoCowboys](/insights/is-nocowboys-worth-it/).

Consistency matters more than quantity. Two hundred listings with three different phone
numbers is worse than fifteen that all match. If you've changed your number or moved, hunt
down the old ones.

### 5. Engagement signals

Clicks, calls, direction requests and photo views feed back into how Google ranks you, and
they've climbed in importance. You can't fake them, but you can earn them: the profile with
twenty recent real photos and forty reviews gets clicked over the one with a logo and six,
and that click is itself a ranking signal. It compounds.

## What doesn't work, and what gets you suspended

- **Keyword stuffing the business name.** "AAA Napier Plumbing Emergency 24/7" does work
  short-term, which is exactly why you keep seeing it. It's also against the guidelines, and
  any competitor can report it in thirty seconds.
- **A fake address, a mate's garage, or a virtual office** to get a pin in another town.
- **Multiple profiles for one business** to cover more suburbs. Use service areas.
- **Buying reviews, or gating them** so only happy customers get asked. Both are policy
  breaches, and removal takes the whole run of them, not just the bought ones.

A suspended profile takes weeks to get reinstated, if it comes back. There is no version of
this where the shortcut is worth it for a business that plans to still be trading in 2030.

## When a competitor is cheating

Sometimes the reason you're fourth is that positions one and three are a keyword-stuffed
name and a listing at a house that has never had a business in it.

You have two tools. **Suggest an edit** on their profile handles obvious name stuffing, and
occasionally works within days. For the rest, Google's **Business Redressal Complaint
Form** is the proper channel for misleading names, addresses and phone numbers. Document it
with screenshots and a clear description of the guideline being broken.

Neither is fast, and neither is guaranteed. Report it, then get back to the work you control.

## The 20-minute diagnosis

Run these in order the next time you're wondering why the phone's quiet:

1. **Get a real picture.** Grid tool, or three screenshots from three suburbs. Where are you
   actually green?
2. **Compare the three above you.** Their primary category, their review count, their review
   recency, whether the owner replies, how many photos, how old the business is.
3. **Find the single biggest gap.** It's usually one of two things: they have four times
   your reviews, or they're closer to the searcher than you and always will be.
4. **Act on the gap, not the checklist.** If it's reviews, that's a twelve-week job you start
   today. If it's distance, no amount of profile work fixes it and the answer is ads or a
   wider radius earned over time.

## The honest ceiling

A perfect profile plus steady reviews plus a real website will put most trade businesses in
the map pack for their own patch, usually within a few months. That's a genuinely great
outcome and it's free apart from your time.

What it won't do is beat a competitor with eleven years, four hundred reviews and an address
two streets from the searcher. Distance is real, prominence takes time, and any agency
promising you the top three across a whole region by Christmas is selling something.

The businesses that win at this are boringly consistent: they ask every customer for a
review, they reply to every one, they put photos up, and they do it for two years while the
competition does it for two months.

## Google map pack FAQ

### What is the map pack, or 3-pack?

The block of three business listings with the map that sits above the normal blue links on a
local search. It's the same set of results you get in Google Maps, presented in Search.

### Why do I show up at home but not across town?

Distance is one of Google's three ranking factors, and the map pack is built around wherever
the searcher is standing. Being visible at your own address is normal and tells you almost
nothing about your real reach.

### How long does it take to get into the top three?

Profile fixes like categories and services can move things within days to weeks. Prominence
work, which is mostly reviews and your website, is a three to six month job in a competitive
town, longer if you're starting from nothing.

### Does my website affect my map pack ranking?

Yes. Google reads the site your profile links to when it works out relevance, so real
service pages and real town pages help the profile rank, not just the site.

### Do I need a physical address to rank?

No. Service area businesses can rank and most tradies should hide their address. You still
verify with a real one, and you're still anchored to it, which is why proximity limits apply.

### Can I pay to be in the map pack?

Not in the organic three. Google does sell an ad slot that appears above or within the map
block, but the three results themselves aren't for sale, whatever anyone tells you on
LinkedIn.
