You can build your own website in a weekend for less than the price of a decent drill.
That is genuinely true, and for some trade businesses it is the right call.

Our interest, up front: we build websites for trade businesses, so we are the paid
alternative to everything on this page. That means we have every reason to talk you out
of a website builder. We are not going to, because for a fair number of tradies a builder
is the sensible answer, and pretending otherwise would be easy to see through.

What we will do is be specific about the one thing builders do not solve, and about the
cost most people forget to count.

## The international options

**Wix** and **Squarespace** are the two giants, and both are perfectly capable of
producing a good-looking trade website. Wix is the more flexible and the more forgiving if
you are not design-minded; Squarespace is the tidier out of the box and harder to make
look bad.

We are not quoting their prices here, because both price in their own currency, run
near-constant promotions and change tiers often. Whatever a blog tells you their plan
costs, check the live page. That applies to this blog too.

The real trade-off with both is support. When something breaks at 7pm on a Sunday and
your quote form has stopped emailing you, you are in a help centre and a ticket queue,
not on the phone to someone in Hamilton.

## What builders genuinely do well

Credit where it is due. A modern website builder will get you:

- A site that looks professional, on a phone, without design skills
- Hosting, security certificates and updates handled
- A domain, usually free for the first year
- Something live this week rather than next month
- A monthly cost you can cancel any time

For a sole trader, a part-timer, someone testing a new service line, or anyone whose work
comes almost entirely from referrals and just needs somewhere to point people, that is
enough. Genuinely. Spend the money you saved on getting reviews instead.

## The bit builders do not do

Here is the gap, and it is the same gap in all of them.

A website builder builds a **website**. It does not answer the enquiry.

When someone fills in your form at 8pm, a builder site sends you an email. That is the
whole job done. Whether that person hears back tonight, tomorrow, or on Saturday when you
finally get to the laptop, is entirely on you. And the tradie who replies first wins a
large share of those jobs, because most people ring two or three businesses and go with
whoever gets back to them.

That is not a criticism of the software. Replying to enquiries was never what it was sold
to do. But it is the difference between a website and a system that gets you work, and it
is the reason we build what we build. Our sites reply the moment an enquiry lands, ask the
questions that qualify the job, and either book it in or hand you a callback with the
details already gathered.

If you go the builder route, solve the follow-up separately. Even a phone reminder and a
rule that every enquiry gets answered within the hour will beat most of your competitors.

## The cost everyone forgets

The sticker price is not the cost. Your time is.

Building your own site properly, with your services written out, real photos of your work,
sensible page structure and a form that actually works, takes most people somewhere
between 20 and 40 hours. First-timers, more.

Put your own charge-out rate against that. At $85 an hour, 30 hours of your evenings is
**$2,550** of billable time you did not bill. At $110 an hour it is $3,300. That is before
you count the jobs you did not quote because you were fighting a template.

Now, that maths only holds if those hours would otherwise have been earning. If you are
quiet, they would not have been, and building it yourself is close to free. That is
exactly why the answer is different for a tradie with a full calendar and a tradie with a
gap.

The rule that falls out of it:

- **Busy, work coming in, no time.** Your hours are worth more on the tools. Hire it out.
- **Quiet, watching every dollar, time on your hands.** Build it yourself. Come back to us
  when the phone is ringing.

## Which one, for which tradie

**Sole trader, mostly referrals, needs a shopfront.** Rocketspark. NZ support, fair price,
you will have it live in a weekend.

**Design-conscious, wants it to look sharp, has patience.** Squarespace.

**Wants maximum control and does not mind fiddling.** Wix.

**Has staff, real ad spend, and enquiries that must not be missed.** A builder will not
cover you here. The site is only a third of the problem; the follow-up and the ads are the
rest.

**Already has a builder site and it is not producing work.** The site is rarely the
problem on its own. Check three things first: does it say clearly what you do and where,
does it load fast on a phone, and does every enquiry get answered same day. Fix those
before you spend on a rebuild.

## Website builder FAQ

### What is the best website builder in New Zealand?

For most trade businesses, Rocketspark, on the strength of being NZ-built with NZ support
and NZD billing. Wix and Squarespace are both more powerful and cheaper to start; you
trade away local support. There is no single best, only the one that fits how much time
you have.

### How much does a website builder cost in NZ?

Rocketspark starts at $49 +GST a month as at 18/08/2026. The international builders vary
by plan and promotion, so check their live pricing. Add a domain (roughly $30 to $60 a
year) and business email if it is not bundled. For what a fully built site costs instead,
see our guide to [what a website costs in NZ](/insights/website-cost-nz/).

### Can I build a trade website myself?

Yes. Thousands of tradies have. Budget 20 to 40 hours, get real photos of your own work
rather than stock images, and make sure your phone number is on every page. The most
common mistake is a beautiful site that never says which towns you cover.

### Is a website builder bad for SEO?

No. All the major builders produce sites Google can read perfectly well. Rankings come
down to your content, your Google Business Profile and your reviews far more than your
platform. If you want the detail, that is what our [SEO services](/seo/) page covers.

### Should I use a builder or hire someone?

Compare the build cost against your own charge-out rate times the hours it will take you.
If you are busy, hiring it out is usually cheaper in real terms. If you are quiet, build
it yourself.

### Will a website builder get me more jobs?

On its own, not really. It gives people somewhere to land and something to judge you by.
Getting found is [SEO and ads](/lead-generation/), and converting the enquiry is
follow-up. The website is the middle piece, not the whole thing.
