Ask three companies what a website costs in New Zealand and you'll get three numbers so
far apart they don't seem to be describing the same thing: $79 a month here, $4,000 there,
$15,000 from the agency in town. None of them are lying. They're quoting different products
that happen to share a name.

Here's what websites actually cost in NZ in 2026, what you get at each price, and the
ongoing costs that don't make it into the quote.

## The short answer: NZ website prices in 2026

| What you're buying | Typical upfront cost | Typical ongoing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DIY website builder (Wix, Squarespace and similar) | $0 | $20 to $60/mo plus your evenings |
| Freelancer template build | $500 to $3,000 | hosting, roughly $10 to $50/mo |
| Small studio, semi-custom | $2,000 to $6,000 | hosting plus support, varies |
| Agency custom build | $5,000 to $20,000+ | $100 to $300/mo maintenance |
| Large agency or enterprise | $20,000 to $100,000+ | retainer |

These are the ranges we see in the NZ market as at July 2026. Individual quotes will land
outside them for good and bad reasons, which is what the rest of this article unpacks.

## What you actually get at each price level

**DIY builders ($0 upfront, $20 to $60 a month).** You get a template and a login. The
build cost is your own time: most business owners spend 20 to 40 hours getting something
they're half happy with. The bigger catch isn't the design, it's that a website nobody
visits earns nothing, and the builders don't bring visitors. Fine for a hobby or a
side gig. Risky as the front door of a real business.

**Freelancers ($500 to $3,000).** This range varies more than any other. At its best you
get a sharp operator who delivers a clean, fast site for a fair price. At its worst you
get the classic NZ story: the site goes up, the freelancer moves on or disappears, and two
years later you can't get into your own domain account. If you go this route, make sure
the domain is registered in **your** name and you hold the logins. That one sentence has
saved businesses thousands.

**Studios and agencies ($2,000 to $20,000+).** The price jump buys things that aren't
visible in a screenshot: the words on the page written to sell, pages structured around
the services and areas you want to rank for, conversion tracking so you know which
enquiries came from where, and someone contractually still there next year. Whether a
given agency actually delivers those things is worth checking before you sign, because
the invoice alone doesn't guarantee it.

## The ongoing costs nobody puts in the quote

Every website carries running costs, whoever builds it:

- **Domain name:** roughly $25 to $40 a year for a .co.nz or .nz.
- **Hosting:** $10 to $60 a month for most business sites.
- **Email:** if you want yourname@yourbusiness.co.nz through Google or Microsoft, about
  $10 to $25 per person per month.
- **SSL certificate:** should be free and included. If someone's charging you for the
  padlock, ask why.
- **Changes and updates:** this is the quiet one. Cheap builds often come with $150-an-hour
  change fees, so the $800 website costs $2,000 by the end of year one. Ask what a simple
  text change costs before you commit.

A realistic floor for keeping a professional site alive is around $300 to $800 a year
before any marketing. With a maintenance retainer, $1,500 to $4,000 a year is common.

## The real question: who is responsible for it working?

DIY, freelancer or agency is really a question about accountability. When the site loads
slowly, drops off Google or stops sending enquiries, who owns that problem?

- With **DIY**, it's you, at 9pm, on YouTube tutorials.
- With a **freelancer**, it's whoever answers the email, if they still do this work.
- With an **agency or studio**, it should be them, in writing. That accountability is a
  big part of what the higher price buys.

None of these answers is wrong. They suit different stages of business. The mistake is
paying agency money and getting DIY accountability, and plenty of quotes are structured
exactly that way: a build fee, a handover, and good luck.

## What a website needs before it can produce leads

Whatever you pay, a website earns its keep only if it does five jobs:

1. **Loads fast on a phone.** Most of your visitors are on mobile, often mid-task. Slow
   sites lose them before the first word.
2. **Says what you do and where, immediately.** A page per service and a clear service
   area. This is also what Google ranks you on.
3. **Makes contact effortless.** A short quote form and a phone number that works in one
   tap, not a contact page hidden three clicks deep.
4. **Replies instantly.** An enquiry answered straight away, at any hour, beats a better
   quote sent tomorrow. Automatic email replies cost almost nothing and win real jobs.
5. **Tracks where enquiries come from.** Without tracking, you can't tell which dollars
   are working, and every marketing decision after that is a guess.

If you're getting quotes, put those five in front of every provider and watch which ones
have real answers.

## Website cost FAQ

### How much should you pay for a basic website in NZ?

For a professional few-page business site built by someone else, expect $500 to $3,000.
Below that you're buying a template you could set up yourself; well above it, you should
be getting copywriting, SEO structure and tracking, not just design.

### How much do website designers charge per hour in NZ?

Most NZ freelance designers and developers charge $90 to $180 an hour in 2026, with
senior specialists above that. A "cheap" fixed quote at a high hourly rate often just
means fewer hours: fair enough, as long as you know what got left out.

### Is a website worth the money?

Only if people can find it and it's built to turn visitors into enquiries. A beautiful
site nobody visits is a brochure in a drawer. Budget for the site and for getting people
to it, or the site alone will disappoint you.

### How much does it cost to maintain a website in NZ?

Domain, hosting and email land around $300 to $800 a year. Add a maintenance or support
retainer and $1,500 to $4,000 a year is typical. Always ask what routine content changes
cost, because that's where cheap builds get expensive.

### Why do quotes vary so much for the same website?

Because "website" hides the real deliverables: custom design versus template, who writes
the copy, whether SEO structure and conversion tracking are included, and who supports it
after launch. Get every quote itemised on those four and they suddenly become comparable.

### Can I get a website for free?

Sort of. Free tiers of the DIY builders put your business on their subdomain with their
ads. That's fine for testing an idea, but it looks like what it is, and you can't run
serious marketing on it. For a trading business, the free option is the dearest one.
